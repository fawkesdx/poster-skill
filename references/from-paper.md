# From a paper to a poster

Use this when the source is a paper, preprint, or abstract. The poster is a new sheet. It is not the paper, scaled down.

## What to read, and in this order

1. Title and abstract.
2. Figure captions, then the figures they name.
3. The last section.
4. Methods only for steps that will appear on the sheet.

Stop there unless a number on the sheet is still unsourced. Do not walk the whole paper onto the poster.

## Compression

| In the paper | On the poster |
|---|---|
| Abstract | One takeaway sentence and 3–6 key ideas |
| Opening sections | One line of context, or nothing |
| Methods | A flow of 3–5 boxes |
| The figure that carries the claim | One evidence region, cropped |
| Other figures | Off the sheet, unless one of them is the comparison |
| Limits and caveats | The must-not list. One footer line if a reader would otherwise leave with the wrong claim |
| Bibliography | A short link the user supplied, or nothing. Not a reference list |

## Claims

Copy numbers. Do not round them into a stronger claim, and do not invent a score the source never gives.

- If the source says the comparison is qualitative, the poster states no agreement score.
- If the source gives two counts, those counts stay exact.
- If a parameter is not needed to see the claim, it goes in `docs/notes.md`, not on the sheet.
- If you cannot find a source location for a value, leave the value off the sheet.

Each on-sheet value gets a facts row: the value, and where it lives (section, figure, table, or page).

## Figures from the paper

Crop to the claim. Cut panels the poster does not use. Keep the data. Do not redraw a curve, map, or bar from memory. If you cannot crop the real image, omit it and ask for the pictorial idea instead of inventing the panel. The pictorial idea goes to the figure subagent when that mode is available, and otherwise the handoff file is for the user to pass on. The paper file does not go either way.

A picture of a person, a medical image, or a result the user has not said can be shown stays off the sheet until they clear that item.

Split a kept figure into its objects, and leave those objects blank of type. Captions and tiny annotations come off the image. The short labels go on the poster, at poster size, when the sheet is arranged.

Do not carry the paper's graphic identity: journal template, publisher mark, or the journal's colour. Author names go on the poster as text, each linked to the institution the user named for that person. Do not lift a mark out of the PDF. Search for the official logo of each linked institution.

## What the speaker keeps

`docs/notes.md` holds the sentences you refused to print: parameters, the limits, what you left off, and where each placed image came from.

## Then the same draft loop

The compression above is the story. Draw it one block at a time, with a proof they can look at, as in [draft-loop.md](draft-loop.md). Do not paste the paper onto the sheet in one pass.
